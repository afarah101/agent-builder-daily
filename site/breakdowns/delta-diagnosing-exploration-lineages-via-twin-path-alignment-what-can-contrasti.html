DELTA: Diagnosing Exploration Lineages via Twin-path Alignment - What Can Contrasting Design Paths Reveal About Microarchitectural Behavior?

Summary

DELTA is a systematic framework for capturing every microarchitectural design decision made during a processor or system exploration cycle. The study tracked parent‑child intervention pairs—each time a design tweak was applied, DELTA logged the change, the resulting performance metrics, and the surrounding configuration context. By building a directed acyclic graph of these interventions, the authors were able to compute a twin‑path alignment score that quantifies the similarity between two design trajectories. The key finding is that contrasting design paths that diverge early but converge later often expose hidden dependencies in the microarchitecture. When the alignment score was high, the system could predict the impact of a new tweak with 92 % accuracy, and root‑cause analysis revealed that 68 % of performance regressions were due to cache‑line contention or branch‑prediction mis‑tuning rather than the intended change.

For enterprise IT, this methodology translates to a data‑driven approach to performance tuning in production workloads. Traditional ad‑hoc tuning relies on isolated benchmarks; DELTA provides a historical map that shows why a particular cache‑line size or prefetcher setting helped one workload but hurt another. The real‑world implication is a measurable IPC improvement of up to 1.4 % on average across mixed‑workload servers, which, when scaled to a data‑center, can reduce CPU utilization by 3–5 % and lower power costs. Moreover, the alignment framework can surface microarchitectural bottlenecks that are invisible to standard profiling tools, enabling proactive hardware‑software co‑design in virtualized environments.
