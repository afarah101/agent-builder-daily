Detailed summary

CriticNPU is a design‑space exploration framework aimed at accelerating the development of neural processing units (NPUs) for large‑language‑model (LLM) inference. The authors evaluated a two‑stage workflow: a lightweight, physics‑based surrogate model that predicts the relative performance of candidate hardware configurations, followed by a selective refinement stage that runs full‑scale simulations only on promising candidates. In a series of benchmark workloads, the surrogate correctly rejected 45–60 % of sub‑optimal designs, reducing the number of expensive simulator runs by roughly 50 %. The final refined designs matched or surpassed the best latency achieved by exhaustive search, with an average latency improvement of 12 % over baseline configurations. For enterprise IT, this translates to a measurable reduction in design cycle time, lower engineering effort, and faster time‑to‑market for custom AI accelerators. The study also demonstrates that the surrogate model can be trained on a modest dataset (≈200 simulations), making it practical for organizations that lack extensive simulation infrastructure. Real‑world implications include the ability to rapidly prototype NPUs tailored to specific LLM workloads, more efficient allocation of cloud AI resources, and the potential to embed the surrogate into continuous integration pipelines for hardware‑software co‑design. By cutting simulation costs in half, enterprises can reallocate budget toward higher‑value tasks such as algorithmic optimization or data pipeline engineering, while maintaining competitive inference performance.
