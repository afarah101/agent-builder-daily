CPU-ARC: Agentic Reasoning Caching for on-CPU Reasoning Reuse

Summary

CPU‑ARC is a research effort that converts the reasoning steps performed by a large‑language‑model (LLM) into executable CPU code that can be cached and reused. The study evaluated the approach on a suite of mathematics and science problems, measuring both accuracy and cost. Results show that the cached reasoning traces achieve nearly the same accuracy as the original LLM while reducing inference cost by 16‑to‑291×. The authors implemented a lightweight cache that stores intermediate reasoning traces and re‑uses them for new inputs, enabling continuous training and explainability. For enterprise IT, this means that workloads that previously required expensive GPU‑based LLM calls can be migrated to commodity CPU infrastructure, lowering capital and operational expenditures. The real‑world implications include the ability to run large‑scale reasoning workloads on existing data‑center hardware, improved auditability of decision logic, and the potential to integrate LLM reasoning into mission‑critical pipelines without vendor lock‑in. The study also demonstrates that a caching layer can be built on top of existing inference engines, providing a clear path for incremental adoption in regulated environments where explainability and cost control are paramount.
