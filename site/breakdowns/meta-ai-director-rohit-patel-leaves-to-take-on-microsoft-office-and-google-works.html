Meta AI director Rohit Patel leaves to take on Microsoft Office and Google Workspace

Summary

Meta AI director Rohit Patel has departed Meta to launch Ndoc, an AI‑native workspace positioned as a direct competitor to Microsoft Office and Google Workspace. Ndoc consolidates spreadsheets, presentations, dashboards, and code into a single interface, offering AI‑generated content, transparent Python scripts, and a unified .ndoc.html export format. The platform claims to streamline collaboration by eliminating manual data transfer between tools and providing audit trails for AI‑generated content. For enterprise IT teams, this development is significant because it introduces a new vendor that could replace or supplement existing productivity suites, potentially reducing licensing costs and simplifying the software stack. Ndoc’s integration with single sign‑on (SSO) and data‑sharing capabilities means it will interact directly with identity and device management systems such as JumpCloud and Okta, requiring careful assessment of authentication, data residency, and compliance implications. The broader industry implication is a shift toward AI‑centric productivity platforms that promise tighter integration of data and code, which may accelerate the move away from legacy Office and Workspace deployments. Vendors that could be impacted include Microsoft (Office 365), Google (Workspace), and any third‑party add‑ons that rely on the Office or Google APIs. IT teams should evaluate whether Ndoc’s AI features and unified format provide tangible benefits over current solutions, and whether the platform can be securely integrated into existing MDM, SSO, and compliance frameworks.
