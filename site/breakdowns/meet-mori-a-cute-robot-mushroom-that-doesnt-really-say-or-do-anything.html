Mori is marketed as a decorative, mood‑enhancing gadget rather than a functional AI assistant. The announcement highlights a growing trend of “ambient intelligence” products that focus on user experience and emotional well‑being instead of integration with existing digital ecosystems. For enterprise IT teams, the product is largely irrelevant to core infrastructure, security, or productivity tools. However, it illustrates a broader industry shift toward niche, experience‑centric devices that do not require cloud connectivity or data sharing. This trend can influence procurement decisions, as organizations may need to evaluate whether such devices pose any security or compliance risks when deployed in corporate environments. Vendors involved include OKA as the manufacturer, and the product likely relies on proprietary firmware and Bluetooth Low Energy for motion detection. IT engineer application guide

Evaluate

- Inventory all IoT devices currently in use; identify any that lack vendor support or firmware update paths. - Verify whether devices have open network interfaces (Wi‑Fi, Bluetooth) that could be exploited. - Assess the device’s data handling: does it collect or transmit user data beyond the local environment?

Propose

- Build a cost‑benefit analysis comparing the device’s purchase price, potential productivity impact, and any security remediation costs. - Use metrics such as mean time to detect (MTTD) for IoT vulnerabilities and potential downtime if a device is compromised. - Highlight the lack of integration with existing security tools (e.g., JumpCloud, Freshservice) as a risk factor.

Tools to consider

- IoT security platforms: Cisco IoT Threat Defense, Palo Alto Networks IoT Security, or ForeScout CounterACT. - Network segmentation solutions: Cisco Meraki MX, Fortinet FortiGate, or Ubiquiti UniFi. - Device management: Microsoft Intune for IoT, VMware Workspace ONE, or open‑source solutions like OpenWrt for custom firmware.

Risks to flag

- Technical: Unpatched firmware, open Bluetooth interfaces, lack of encryption. - Compliance: Potential violation of UK GDPR if the device collects location or motion data without proper consent. - Operational: Device may interfere with existing network traffic or consume bandwidth.

Quick win

- Deploy a network‑level firewall rule to block all outbound traffic from the device’s MAC address, ensuring it cannot communicate with external servers. - Conduct a one‑hour training session for end users on safe usage and reporting procedures.

Long-term play

- Over 6–12 months, develop an IoT governance framework that includes device classification, risk scoring, and lifecycle management. - Integrate IoT device monitoring into existing SIEM (e.g., Splunk, ELK) to detect anomalous behavior. - Evaluate vendor support contracts and establish a process for decommissioning non‑essential IoT devices to maintain a secure, compliant environment.
