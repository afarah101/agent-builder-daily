Physical AI is more than robots, says the Nvidia veteran now investing in it

TechCrunch · 16 Oct 202 · Generated 16 Oct 2026, 20:51

Summary

Amulya Vishwanath, a former NVIDIA robotics product lead, recently stated that robotics remains in a “Lego era,” meaning that the industry is still building foundational components such as actuators, grippers, control software, and safety standards. He argues that the anticipated wave of general foundation models—large AI systems that could be applied across many domains—has not yet materialized for robotics. Vishwanath’s comments come as NVIDIA continues to invest in physical AI, a term that encompasses the integration of AI with physical systems like robots. The key takeaway for enterprise IT teams is that the current robotics landscape is still fragmented, with no single platform or standard that can be immediately integrated into existing MDM (mobile device management) or SSO (single sign‑on) ecosystems. Vendors such as NVIDIA, Boston Dynamics, and Universal Robots are still developing hardware and software stacks, and safety frameworks like ISO 10218 or IEC 61508 are still evolving. For IT, this means that any upcoming robotic APIs or device management extensions will likely require custom integration work and may introduce new security and compliance requirements. The broader industry implication is that enterprises should not expect a plug‑and‑play robotics solution in the near term; instead, they should monitor the maturation of component standards, evaluate how robotic endpoints might be added to existing device pools, and prepare for the eventual need to extend MDM, identity, and security controls to physical devices. The lack of a unified foundation model for robotics also suggests that vendors will continue to compete on hardware capabilities and proprietary software, making vendor lock‑in a potential risk for organizations that adopt early.

How to apply

Evaluate

- Inventory current endpoint management scope: list all devices, OS versions, and existing MDM/SSO integrations. - Identify potential robotic endpoints: assess whether any industrial robots, warehouse AGVs, or service robots are planned for deployment. - Map data flows: determine how robotic devices would exchange data with corporate networks, cloud services, and identity providers. - Review compliance posture: check UK GDPR implications for data captured by robots (e.g., video, sensor logs).

Propose

- Build a cost‑benefit model: estimate savings from automating repetitive tasks versus the cost of integrating robots into MDM. - Benchmark security metrics: use NIST CSF or ISO 27001 controls to quantify risk reduction from centralized device management. - Present a phased ROI: show incremental gains over 6–12 months, including reduced support tickets and improved audit coverage.

Tools to consider

- VMware Workspace ONE or Microsoft Intune for extending MDM to non‑traditional devices. - Cisco Secure Endpoint (formerly AMP) for adding robotic endpoints to existing endpoint protection. - Open-source robotic middleware such as ROS 2 with security extensions (e.g., ROS 2 Security). - Vendor‑specific SDKs: NVIDIA Isaac SDK, Universal Robots URScript, or Boston Dynamics Spot SDK.

Risks to flag

- Technical: lack of standardized APIs may force custom adapters, increasing maintenance overhead. - Compliance: robots may capture personal data; ensure GDPR data‑subject rights are respected. - Operational: robotic downtime could impact production; lack of remote management may hinder rapid remediation. - Security: new attack surface on physical devices; need for secure boot, firmware integrity, and network segmentation.

Quick win

- Conduct a pilot integration of a single robotic endpoint into the existing MDM platform using a vendor SDK. Configure basic device enrollment, policy enforcement, and remote wipe. Document the process and any gaps in policy coverage within 30 days.

Long-term play

- Over 6–12 months, develop a robotic device management framework that includes: * Standardized onboarding procedures (certificate‑based authentication, secure boot). * Unified policy language that maps to existing MDM policies. * Continuous monitoring and threat detection tailored to robotic firmware and sensor data. * Collaboration with vendors to adopt emerging safety and security standards (ISO 10218, IEC 61508).
