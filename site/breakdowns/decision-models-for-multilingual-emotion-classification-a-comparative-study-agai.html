Decision Models for Multilingual Emotion Classification: A Comparative Study Against LLMs and Encoders

Detailed summary

The study evaluated decision‑based models for multilingual emotion classification against two fine‑tuned multilingual encoders (e.g., XLM‑R, mBERT) and two large language models (LLMs) such as GPT‑4 and Claude. Ten social‑media datasets spanning five languages (English, Spanish, French, German, Italian) were used to benchmark macro‑F1 scores, inference latency, and probability outputs. Decision models—simple rule‑based or lightweight classifiers that map encoded features to emotion labels—achieved macro‑F1 within one percentage point of the best fine‑tuned encoder. They also produced per‑option probability distributions, enabling downstream confidence‑based routing. In zero‑shot scenarios, the best decision models matched the accuracy of a large LLM while running five times faster and requiring no labeled training data.

For enterprise IT, these findings are significant because they demonstrate that high‑quality, multilingual emotion detection can be delivered with minimal compute resources and without the operational overhead of training large models. Faster inference reduces latency for real‑time customer support, sentiment monitoring, and compliance filtering. The ability to expose probability scores allows for dynamic thresholding and human‑in‑the‑loop escalation, improving auditability and trust.

Real‑world implications include the potential to replace or supplement existing LLM‑based sentiment pipelines in customer‑facing applications, thereby cutting GPU usage, energy costs, and vendor lock‑in. Enterprises can deploy decision models on edge devices or low‑cost cloud instances, scaling to millions of messages per day. Additionally, the transparency of probability outputs aids in meeting regulatory requirements for explainability, especially under UK GDPR where automated decision‑making must be interpretable. The study provides a concrete performance baseline that can be used to justify migration from expensive LLMs to lightweight decision models in production environments.
