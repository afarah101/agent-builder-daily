Detailed summary

Shield has updated its Forge platform to integrate OpenAI’s GPT‑6 Astra, a next‑generation large language model that can interact with legacy web portals. The new capability allows AI agents to log in, navigate, and perform routine tasks—such as ticket creation, device provisioning, and license allocation—on systems that historically lacked APIs or structured data access. For enterprise IT teams, this means a measurable reduction in manual portal work, faster ticket resolution, and the ability to reallocate technicians to higher‑value activities. The integration is significant because many support environments still rely on legacy portals for critical functions; automating these interactions can cut response times by up to 30% and lower operational costs. Shield’s Forge platform, now powered by GPT‑6 Astra, competes with other AI‑augmented IT service management solutions such as ServiceNow’s Virtual Agent, BMC Helix, and Freshservice. The broader industry implication is a shift toward “computer‑use” AI agents that can handle unstructured web interfaces, reducing the dependency on custom API development and accelerating digital transformation in support operations. Vendors that provide AI‑driven workflow automation, identity and access management, and ITSM will need to adapt to this new paradigm, potentially offering similar portal‑automation modules or partnering with AI providers. Enterprises that fail to evaluate Shield’s solution risk missing out on productivity gains and may lag behind competitors that adopt AI‑enabled portal automation earlier.

IT engineer application guide

Evaluate

• Inventory legacy portals: list URLs, authentication methods, and the volume of daily interactions. • Measure current ticketing metrics: average resolution time, technician hours spent on portal tasks, and error rates. • Assess security posture: determine if portal credentials are stored in password managers or shared via insecure channels. • Map integration gaps: identify which systems lack APIs and the frequency of manual updates.

Propose

• Build a cost‑benefit model: compare current technician hours on portal tasks against projected savings from AI automation. • Use benchmarks: industry studies show a 25‑35% reduction in ticket turnaround time with AI agents. • Highlight ROI: calculate payback period based on reduced labor costs and potential SLA improvements. • Present a phased pilot: start with a single high‑volume portal to demonstrate impact before scaling.

Tools to consider

• Shield Forge with GPT‑6 Astra (primary solution). • ServiceNow Virtual Agent (for comparison). • BMC Helix Digital Workplace. • UiPath or Automation Anywhere (for broader RPA integration). • Okta or Azure AD for secure credential management.

Risks to flag

• Technical: authentication failures, session timeouts, or changes in portal UI that break the AI workflow. • Compliance: ensure credential handling meets UK GDPR, especially if the AI stores or transmits login data. • Operational: overreliance on AI could mask underlying portal issues; maintain human oversight for error handling. • Vendor lock‑in: evaluate Shield’s licensing terms and data residency options.

Quick win

• Deploy Shield’s GPT‑6 Astra on a single, high‑volume portal (e.g., ticketing system). • Configure credential vaulting with Azure Key Vault or HashiCorp Vault. • Run a 30‑day pilot, collect metrics on time saved, and report findings to stakeholders.

Long-term play

• Scale AI agents across all legacy portals, integrating with existing ITSM workflows. • Implement continuous monitoring and retraining of the GPT‑6 model to adapt to UI changes. • Develop a governance framework for AI interactions, including audit trails and exception handling. • Explore cross‑vendor integration (e.g., Shield + ServiceNow) to unify incident, change, and asset management under a single AI‑driven interface.
