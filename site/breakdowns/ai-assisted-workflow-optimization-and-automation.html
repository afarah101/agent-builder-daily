Summary

The study by Zhen Zhong from an academic institution investigates the persistent bottleneck in enterprise compliance operations caused by repetitive, rule‑driven tasks. Using a hybrid architecture that combines robotic process automation (RPA), a rule engine, and semantic recognition, the authors re‑engineered auxiliary compliance workflows such as data classification, audit trail generation, and regulatory reporting. The framework was deployed in a mid‑size financial services firm with 1,200 employees and 15,000 daily transaction records. Key findings include a 45 % reduction in manual processing time, a 60 % decrease in error rates, and a 30 % improvement in audit readiness scores. The system’s semantic layer automatically tagged documents with regulatory context, enabling real‑time compliance status dashboards. For enterprise IT, the study demonstrates that integrating RPA with a declarative rule engine can transform legacy compliance processes into a modular, auditable pipeline. The real‑world implications are significant: faster cycle times reduce regulatory fines, clearer audit trails improve governance, and a risk‑controlled architecture allows for rapid scaling to new jurisdictions. The approach also provides a blueprint for extending automation to other operational domains such as procurement and HR, where repetitive compliance checks are common. Overall, the case study validates that a structured, technology‑driven workflow redesign can deliver measurable compliance and operational benefits without extensive re‑engineering of core business systems.
