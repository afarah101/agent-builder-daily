Detailed summary

MaskPE is a hardware/software co‑design that addresses the mismatch between weight pruning in deep neural networks and the energy savings realized on systolic array accelerators. The authors investigated how the presence of zero weights in compressed models leads to wasted memory bandwidth and unnecessary switching activity in the array. They introduced a dual‑purpose mask processor that routes non‑zero weights to the processing elements (PEs) while simultaneously clock‑gating the PEs that would otherwise multiply by zero. The study was performed on a 64×64 systolic array prototype, using a mix of convolutional and fully connected layers from standard benchmarks such as ImageNet and CIFAR‑10. The key findings are: (1) compressed weight storage can be reduced by 34.9% because the mask processor eliminates the need to store zero entries; (2) execution energy drops by up to 14.4% at 50% sparsity, with the benefit scaling with higher sparsity levels; (3) the additional logic for mask routing adds less than 2% area overhead and does not impact the critical path. For enterprise IT, these results translate into lower data center power consumption, reduced cooling costs, and the ability to deploy larger models on edge devices without exceeding power budgets. The real‑world implications include the potential to extend the lifespan of existing GPU clusters by integrating MaskPE‑compatible accelerators, to accelerate inference workloads for AI‑enabled services, and to meet regulatory energy‑efficiency targets in data centers. The approach also offers a pathway to comply with emerging AI‑specific carbon‑footprint reporting standards, as the energy savings can be quantified and reported in a standardized manner.
