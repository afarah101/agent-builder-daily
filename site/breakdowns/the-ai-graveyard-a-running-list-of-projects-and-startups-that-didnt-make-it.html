Detailed Summary

Relay, an AI‑powered workflow automation platform that positioned itself as a competitor to Zapier, announced its shutdown in August. The company had attracted significant venture capital and built a user base that leveraged its AI‑driven connectors to automate repetitive tasks across SaaS applications. Its exit underscores a pattern in the enterprise AI landscape: a high failure rate, with 42% of corporate AI projects ultimately not sustaining long‑term viability. The collapse is symptomatic of larger ecosystem players—OpenAI, Google, Apple, Microsoft—integrating AI capabilities directly into their native productivity suites, thereby reducing the market for standalone AI workflow tools. For enterprise IT teams, Relay’s demise signals that reliance on third‑party AI services can expose organizations to sudden discontinuity, security gaps, and integration headaches. Vendors such as Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, and Salesforce are rapidly expanding their AI toolsets, offering native automation, natural language processing, and predictive analytics that can replace many of the use cases previously served by Relay. The broader industry implication is a consolidation trend: AI features are moving from niche startups to mainstream platforms, forcing enterprises to evaluate whether to adopt built‑in solutions or maintain specialized third‑party tools. This shift also raises concerns around data residency, compliance, and vendor lock‑in, as organizations must now weigh the trade‑offs of embedding AI within their core productivity ecosystems versus maintaining independent, potentially more flexible, but riskier solutions. The trend highlights the need for IT teams to audit current AI dependencies, assess the risk of vendor abandonment, and strategically plan migration paths to platform‑native AI services to ensure continuity, security, and cost efficiency.

IT Engineer Application Guide
EVALUATE
- Inventory all AI‑driven workflow tools in use (e.g., Relay, Tray.io, Integromat). - Map each tool’s integration points, data flows, and critical business functions. - Assess data residency, encryption, and compliance status (GDPR, UK Data Protection Act). - Identify any single points of failure or unsupported endpoints.
PROPOSE
- Build a cost‑benefit analysis: current subscription spend vs. projected cost of migrating to native AI (e.g., Google Workspace AI, Microsoft Power Automate). - Include metrics: reduction in ticket volume, time‑to‑completion for automated tasks, and ROI from decreased vendor support costs. - Benchmark against industry averages (e.g., 30% reduction in manual task time reported by Microsoft Power Automate adopters). - Present risk mitigation plan for potential vendor shutdowns.
TOOLS TO CONSIDER
- Google Workspace AI (Smart Compose, Smart Reply, AI‑powered data insights). - Microsoft 365 Copilot and Power Automate. - Salesforce Einstein for CRM automation. - Zapier (if still viable) as a fallback for niche connectors. - OpenAI API for custom model integration if needed.
RISKS TO FLAG
- Technical: API deprecation, data format changes, latency issues. - Compliance: GDPR/UK GDPR data transfer to cloud providers, audit trail integrity. - Operational: Staff skill gaps in new platform, change management resistance, potential downtime during migration.
QUICK WIN
- Deploy a pilot automation using Microsoft Power Automate to replace a high‑volume, repetitive task (e.g., auto‑generation of status reports). - Complete within 30 days, validate with end‑user feedback, and measure time savings.
LONG-TERM PLAY
- Over 6–12 months, phase out all third‑party AI workflow tools. - Migrate remaining processes to platform‑native AI solutions, ensuring full integration with existing identity and access management. - Establish a governance framework for AI usage, including model monitoring, bias assessment, and periodic compliance reviews. - Train IT staff on AI governance and platform administration to sustain operations and future innovation.
