Preserving Utility in Continual LLM Unlearning via Orthogonal Projections

Detailed summary

The study investigates the challenge of repeatedly removing sensitive data from large language models (LLMs) while preserving model utility. Traditional unlearning methods often degrade performance after each deletion, especially when deletions are performed sequentially. The authors propose Continual Orthogonal Projection (COP), a gradient‑projection technique that enforces each deletion to be orthogonal to all previous deletions. By maintaining orthogonality, COP prevents cumulative interference that typically erodes model accuracy. Experiments on standard benchmarks such as GLUE, SQuAD, and domain‑specific datasets demonstrate that COP retains higher downstream task performance compared to state‑of‑the‑art continual‑unlearning approaches, while achieving comparable or superior forgetting metrics measured by deletion success rates and privacy leakage scores. The research is significant for enterprises that deploy LLMs in regulated environments where data must be expunged on request (e.g., GDPR “right to be forgotten”). COP offers a practical, low‑overhead solution that can be integrated into existing fine‑tuning pipelines without requiring full model retraining. Real‑world implications include the ability to comply with data‑removal mandates while maintaining service quality, reducing compute costs associated with retraining, and simplifying audit trails for data deletion. Enterprises can adopt COP to implement a robust data‑removal policy that scales with the volume of deletion requests, ensuring that user privacy obligations do not compromise business value derived from LLMs.

IT engineer application guide

Evaluate

- Audit current LLM deployment: version, fine‑tuning history, data provenance, and deletion logs. - Measure baseline utility: accuracy on key business tasks (e.g., customer support, code generation). - Assess deletion success: run a small set of test deletions and evaluate privacy leakage metrics (e.g., membership inference). - Inventory compliance requirements: GDPR, UK GDPR, CCPA, and any industry‑specific data‑handling rules.

Propose

- Build a cost‑benefit analysis: compute current retraining costs per deletion versus projected COP costs (gradient‑projection overhead). - Include metrics: target retention of ≥90% of baseline accuracy after 10, 20, 50 deletions; deletion latency < 5 s per request. - Highlight risk mitigation: reduced audit complexity, lower compute spend, and faster compliance turnaround. - Present a phased pilot: 3‑month pilot with 100 deletion requests, measure KPI drift, and report to leadership.

Tools to consider

- Hugging Face Transformers + Accelerate for fine‑tuning and projection implementation. - NVIDIA Apex or Intel OpenVINO for mixed‑precision training to reduce GPU usage. - Data‑lineage platforms such as Collibra or Alation to track data sources and deletion status. - Privacy‑audit tools: IBM AI Fairness 360 or OpenAI’s OpenAI API audit logs for leakage assessment.

Risks to flag

- Technical: Orthogonal projection may not fully eliminate all traces of sensitive data; residual leakage could persist. - Compliance: UK GDPR requires demonstrable deletion; need to document projection process and audit results. - Operational: Increased training time per deletion cycle; potential GPU bottlenecks if scaling to thousands of requests. - Model drift: Over‑projection could degrade performance on unseen data; continuous monitoring required.

Quick win

- Implement a prototype COP script on a single GPU for a small LLM (e.g., DistilBERT) and run 10 deletion tests. - Measure accuracy drop and privacy leakage before and after projection. - Document results and share with compliance team within 30 days to demonstrate feasibility.

Long-term play

- Integrate COP into the model lifecycle management pipeline (CI/CD). - Automate deletion requests via an API that triggers orthogonal projection and logs the operation. - Scale to multi‑model environment: apply COP to all in‑house fine‑tuned LLMs, monitor cumulative utility over 6–12 months. - Develop a governance framework that ties deletion approvals, projection logs, and audit evidence into the enterprise data catalog.
