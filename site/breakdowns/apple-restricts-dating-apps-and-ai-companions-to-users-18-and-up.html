Summary

Apple has updated its App Store Review Guidelines to mandate that any application classified as a dating or AI companion service must carry an 18+ rating and must explicitly disclose any location‑sharing capabilities. The new policy will be enforced from November 16, 2026. The change is driven by concerns over minors accessing sexual or romantic content and the potential for location data misuse. For enterprise IT teams, the policy means that any third‑party apps used internally that provide dating or AI companion features must be re‑rated and must include a location‑sharing disclosure in their metadata. Failure to comply will result in removal from the App Store, which can disrupt access to tools that teams rely on for collaboration, communication, or data analysis. Vendors such as Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, and AI companion services like Replika, as well as any in‑house or partner‑built apps that fall under these categories, must review their App Store listings. The broader industry implication is a tightening of content controls on mobile platforms, potentially prompting developers to redesign user flows, adjust privacy settings, and re‑evaluate data collection practices. Enterprises that depend on these apps for employee engagement, wellness programs, or partner integrations will need to audit their app portfolios, update compliance documentation, and possibly seek alternative solutions. The policy also signals a shift toward stricter enforcement of age‑verification and location‑privacy standards across the mobile ecosystem, which could influence future regulatory discussions in the EU, UK, and other jurisdictions.
