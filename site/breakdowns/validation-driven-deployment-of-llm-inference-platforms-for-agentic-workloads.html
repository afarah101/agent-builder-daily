Validation-driven Deployment of LLM Inference Platforms for Agentic Workloads

Shai Gretz, Alon Halfon, Yoav Katz, Lena Dankin · Academic Institution · 2026-11-19 · Generated 21 Nov 2026, 23:00

Summary

The study by Gretz, Halfon, Katz, and Dankin addresses the challenge of deploying large language models (LLMs) for agentic workloads—applications where the model acts autonomously, such as virtual assistants, automated support agents, or decision‑making bots. Traditional validation methods rely on synthetic benchmarks that do not capture the complexity of real user interactions. The authors built an end‑to‑end evaluation pipeline that ingests replayed real‑world traces from production systems and supplements them with synthetic tests. The pipeline measures token throughput, concurrency, latency, and code‑coverage across mixed‑expert models (e.g., combining a base model with specialized adapters). By replaying actual user sessions, the authors demonstrated that the pipeline could reproduce live workload characteristics, uncover hidden code‑coverage gaps, and predict performance under scaling scenarios. The key findings are: (1) captured traces provide a more accurate proxy for production load than synthetic workloads; (2) the pipeline can detect performance regressions and coverage gaps before deployment; (3) the open‑source tools and trace dataset enable teams to benchmark and tune large, mixed‑expert models reliably. For enterprise IT, this means a systematic, repeatable approach to validate LLM deployments, reducing the risk of outages, SLA violations, or compliance breaches. The real‑world implications include faster time‑to‑market for AI features, more predictable scaling costs, and a defensible audit trail that satisfies regulatory requirements such as UK GDPR. By adopting this validation framework, enterprises can confidently integrate LLMs into mission‑critical services while maintaining operational stability and regulatory compliance.
