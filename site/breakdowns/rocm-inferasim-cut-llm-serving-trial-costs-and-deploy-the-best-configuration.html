Detailed summary

The study evaluates ROCm InferaSim, a lightweight simulation and tuning agent designed to accelerate large‑language‑model (LLM) serving configuration in cloud environments. Traditional LLM deployment requires exhaustive GPU‑hour consumption to identify optimal batch size, GPU count, and kernel parameters, often involving thousands of trial runs. InferaSim reduces this search space by measuring key kernel configurations once and extrapolating performance to other setups, achieving within‑10% accuracy of full‑scale trials. The authors demonstrate a 90% reduction in trial time while preserving near‑optimal throughput for LLM inference workloads. The methodology relies on ROCm’s profiling hooks to capture kernel launch metrics, then applies a linear regression model to predict throughput across different batch sizes and GPU counts. The simulation is lightweight enough to run on a single GPU, making it feasible for use in continuous integration pipelines or on‑prem clusters.

For enterprise IT, this translates to significant cost savings in both cloud spend and on‑prem resource allocation. By cutting trial time, teams can iterate faster on model deployment, reduce the number of GPU instances required for testing, and accelerate time‑to‑market for AI services. The 10% accuracy margin is acceptable for most production scenarios, especially when combined with a final sanity check on a small set of real traffic. The approach also aligns with DevOps practices, enabling automated performance regression testing and continuous optimization of inference pipelines.

Real‑world implications include lower operational expenditure on GPU hours, improved utilization of existing hardware, and a more predictable budgeting model for AI workloads. Enterprises can adopt InferaSim to streamline their LLM deployment lifecycle, reduce the skill barrier for tuning inference parameters, and maintain compliance with data‑processing regulations by limiting the exposure of sensitive data during trial runs. The study underscores the viability of simulation‑driven optimization as a standard practice in AI‑heavy workloads, providing a clear path for scaling LLM services without proportional increases in cost or complexity.
