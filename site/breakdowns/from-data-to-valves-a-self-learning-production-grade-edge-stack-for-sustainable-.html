Summary

The study by Abbas Raza Ali, Muhammad Hamza Rehman, Xu Li, and Muhammad Ajmal Siddiqui demonstrates a fully on‑edge AI stack deployed on a single NVIDIA Jetson Orin gateway to predict indoor temperatures and control HVAC valve positions in a hotel environment. Over an 18‑month period, the system achieved sub‑degree temperature prediction accuracy and maintained optimal valve actuation without relying on external cloud services. The architecture integrates a lightweight convolutional neural network for temperature forecasting, a reinforcement‑learning policy for valve control, and a continuous learning loop that retrains the model locally as new sensor data arrives. Key findings include: 1) the edge‑only approach eliminates latency and bandwidth constraints associated with cloud‑based HVAC control; 2) continuous local learning allows the system to adapt to seasonal changes and occupancy patterns, improving energy efficiency by up to 12% compared to baseline rule‑based control; 3) the solution operates within the power envelope of the Jetson Orin, making it suitable for retrofit scenarios in existing buildings. For enterprise IT, this case study validates the feasibility of deploying complex AI workloads on single‑board GPUs in production, reducing dependency on data center resources and mitigating data sovereignty concerns. Real‑world implications are significant: organizations can achieve granular, real‑time environmental control, lower operational costs, and improve occupant comfort without extensive cloud infrastructure. The approach also demonstrates a scalable model for other building automation use cases, such as lighting or security, where edge inference and continuous learning can provide resilience and compliance with data protection regulations.
