Detailed summary

Microsoft Research has released a study that emphasizes the necessity of using synthetic data when evaluating Microsoft 365 Copilot at enterprise scale. The research points out that real organizational data is highly sensitive and that exposing it during AI model testing can lead to accidental leaks and compliance violations. The study recommends that enterprises adopt synthetic data pipelines to simulate realistic workloads while preserving privacy. This shift is critical because Copilot, a generative AI module integrated into Microsoft 365, is becoming a core productivity tool for many organizations. The findings underscore the need for robust data governance frameworks that can manage synthetic data generation, labeling, and lifecycle. For IT teams, this means revisiting the data settings in the Microsoft 365 admin portal to ensure that testing environments are isolated and that synthetic data is the default. Vendors such as Microsoft’s own Azure Purview, Azure Synapse, and third‑party synthetic data generators like Databricks, Hazy, and Mostly AI are now central to the deployment strategy. The broader industry implication is a shift toward privacy‑preserving AI testing practices, which could become a compliance requirement in regulated sectors. Organizations that fail to adopt synthetic data for Copilot evaluation risk exposing confidential information, breaching GDPR or UK GDPR, and facing regulatory penalties. The research also signals that other AI‑enabled Microsoft 365 modules will likely follow similar governance models, making data privacy a foundational pillar for AI adoption in the enterprise.
