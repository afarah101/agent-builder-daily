Summary

Amazon has replaced its internal use of Claude Sonnet 4.6 with the newly launched Strands Decider decision model. The switch was driven by a 98 % reduction in token costs while preserving labeling accuracy for a massive ticketing system that processes thousands of support tickets daily. The decision model, built on a decision‑tree architecture, eliminates the need for large language model inference for routine classification tasks. This move directly impacts the cost structure of Amazon’s internal operations, freeing up budget for higher‑value AI projects. For enterprise IT teams, the news signals a shift toward decision‑model based workflows that can dramatically cut cloud spend while maintaining performance. The primary vendors involved are Anthropic (Claude Sonnet 4.6), Strands (Decider), and AWS (SageMaker and AppConfig). The broader industry implication is that large enterprises will increasingly evaluate whether a pure LLM approach is necessary for routine tasks, or if a lightweight decision model can provide the same accuracy at a fraction of the cost. This trend may accelerate the adoption of hybrid AI pipelines, where a decision model routes requests to an LLM only when needed, optimizing both latency and spend. As cloud providers continue to offer more specialized AI services, IT teams must reassess their AI architecture to avoid unnecessary token consumption and to keep inference times predictable. Failure to adopt cost‑efficient models could result in higher operational expenses and slower ticket resolution, impacting customer satisfaction and service level agreements.
