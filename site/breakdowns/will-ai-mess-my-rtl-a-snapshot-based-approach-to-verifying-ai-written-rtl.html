Will AI mess my RTL? A snapshot-based approach to verifying AI-written RTL

Detailed summary

The study introduces SVApshot, an automated verification framework that leverages large language models (LLMs) to generate and repair SystemVerilog assertions (SVA) for RTL modules. The authors target the growing trend of AI‑assisted RTL design, where code is written or modified by LLMs, raising concerns about functional correctness and regression safety. SVApshot operates by first capturing a snapshot of the current RTL behavior through simulation, then using an LLM to synthesize assertions that describe that behavior. When the RTL is subsequently modified—either by human designers or by another AI pass—the system re‑runs the simulation against the snapshot and automatically detects deviations, generating new assertions or flagging regressions. The evaluation covers several complex RTL blocks, including a cryptographic engine and a memory controller, and shows that SVApshot detects 92% of injected bugs that traditional coverage‑based methods miss, while increasing functional coverage by 18%. The authors argue that this approach mitigates the risk of silent regressions in AI‑generated RTL, a critical issue for enterprises that rely on rapid design cycles and continuous integration pipelines. For enterprise IT, the implications are significant: the ability to automatically verify AI‑written RTL reduces manual verification effort, shortens time‑to‑market, and lowers the probability of costly post‑silicon bugs. Moreover, the snapshot‑based regression suite can be integrated into existing CI/CD workflows, providing a lightweight yet effective safety net for AI‑driven design flows. The study also highlights the need for robust data governance, as the LLMs require access to proprietary RTL code, raising compliance considerations. Overall, SVApshot demonstrates a practical, scalable method to ensure the reliability of AI‑enhanced hardware design, offering a clear pathway for enterprises to adopt AI in RTL development without compromising quality or security.
