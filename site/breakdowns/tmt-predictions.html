Detailed summary

Deloitte released its 2026 TMT Predictions report, projecting a shift toward a quieter but more efficient AI ecosystem. The analysis argues that the next wave of AI deployment will prioritize practical scaling, rigorous data hygiene, robust governance, and cross‑industry collaboration over the flashy, high‑profile models that dominated recent years. According to the report, the technology, media, and telecom sectors will continue to drive economic growth, while AI integration will become a core component of operational efficiency across all industries. The findings suggest that enterprises must focus on establishing solid data foundations and governance frameworks now, or risk falling behind as AI becomes ubiquitous. The report highlights that vendors offering data cataloging, lineage, and governance solutions—such as Collibra, Alation, and Informatica—will see increased demand. Likewise, AI platform providers that embed compliance checkpoints into their workflows, like DataRobot, H2O.ai, and Databricks, will be positioned to capture market share. For IT teams, the key takeaway is that AI adoption will no longer be a technology experiment but a strategic imperative that hinges on data quality and regulatory alignment. Failure to address these prerequisites could expose organizations to compliance violations, especially under UK GDPR and other data protection regimes, and could lead to costly re‑engineering of AI pipelines. The broader industry implication is a convergence of data management and AI operations, where data governance becomes a prerequisite for scalable, compliant AI solutions. Enterprises that proactively audit their data hygiene, embed governance into AI pipelines, and partner with vendors that support these practices will be better positioned to capture the projected growth in the TMT sector.

IT engineer application guide

Evaluate

- Inventory all data sources used for AI projects, noting ownership, quality metrics, and lineage. - Assess current data governance policies for completeness, enforcement, and alignment with UK GDPR (data subject rights, lawful basis, retention schedules). - Review existing AI workflow tools to identify gaps in compliance checkpoints (e.g., bias detection, explainability, audit trails). - Map out data flow from ingestion to model deployment to spot potential leakage or duplication.

Propose

- Build a cost‑benefit analysis that quantifies expected efficiency gains (e.g., 20% reduction in model training time, 15% decrease in data remediation effort). - Use benchmarks from Deloitte’s report: enterprises that improved data hygiene saw a 25% faster AI model rollout. - Include risk mitigation savings: estimate potential fines avoided by meeting GDPR requirements (e.g., £500k per violation). - Present ROI over 12–24 months, highlighting both tangible (cost savings, speed) and intangible (compliance, reputation) benefits.

Tools to consider

- Collibra or Alation for data cataloging and lineage. - Informatica or Talend for data quality and cleansing pipelines. - DataRobot, H2O.ai, or Databricks for AI model development with built‑in governance modules. - Microsoft Purview or AWS Lake Formation for unified data governance across cloud environments. - OpenAI’s compliance‑aware API access for controlled model usage.

Risks to flag

- Technical: Incomplete lineage can lead to model drift; inadequate data quality may produce biased outcomes. - Compliance: Failure to document data processing activities can breach UK GDPR; lack of subject‑rights mechanisms risks fines. - Operational: Over‑centralization of data governance may slow innovation; insufficient training can lead to misuse of AI tools.

- Deploy a data catalog in the next 30 days to surface data assets, classify sensitivity, and flag incomplete metadata. This will provide immediate visibility for governance teams and reduce downstream remediation effort.

Long-term play

- Over 6–12 months, implement an end‑to‑end data governance framework that integrates with AI pipelines: automate data quality checks, embed bias detection, and enforce audit trails.
