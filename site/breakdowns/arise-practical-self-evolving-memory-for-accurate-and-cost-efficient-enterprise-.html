Detailed summary

The ARISE study investigates a self‑evolving memory framework designed to maintain the accuracy of large language model (LLM) agents in dynamic enterprise environments. Traditional fine‑tuning or periodic retraining of LLMs is costly and often impractical for organizations that must adapt to frequent policy changes, new data sources, or evolving user requirements. ARISE addresses this by allowing agents to learn autonomously from their own failures. The system captures micro‑memories—individual failure instances—stores them, and then consolidates these into macro‑rules that represent generalized corrective knowledge. Crucially, changes are only adopted if they demonstrably improve held‑out accuracy, preventing the drift that often plagues continual learning approaches.

Empirical results show a 10‑plus‑point increase in accuracy over a baseline system that does not incorporate self‑learning, and a 4‑point improvement over the GEPA benchmark across fourteen distinct enterprise domains, including finance, HR, and supply chain. Additionally, ARISE reduces model calls by 4% and token usage by 5%, translating into tangible cost savings on cloud inference services. For enterprise IT, these findings are significant because they demonstrate a method to keep LLM agents up‑to‑date without the overhead of full model retraining or the risk of introducing regressions. The ability to autonomously refine behavior also aligns with regulatory demands for traceability and explainability, as micro‑memories can be audited to show how decisions evolved.

Real‑world implications include lower operational expenditures on AI infrastructure, improved compliance posture through auditable learning logs, and faster time‑to‑value for new LLM‑driven applications. Organizations can deploy ARISE to maintain high‑quality conversational agents, automated support bots, or policy‑enforcement tools that must adapt to changing regulations or internal procedures. The approach also offers a framework for continuous improvement that can be integrated with existing CI/CD pipelines, enabling IT teams to manage LLM behavior as part of their standard DevOps workflows.

- Technical: Overfitting to recent failures, potential degradation of older knowledge, increased complexity in version control. - Compliance: Storing user data in micro‑memories may violate UK GDPR if personal data is not adequately anonymized; ensure data minimization and retention policies. - Operational: Increased load on logging infrastructure, risk of stale macro‑rules if not regularly validated, potential latency spikes during consolidation phases.
