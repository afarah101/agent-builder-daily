Detailed summary

In March, a UK jury ruled that Meta and Google were liable for user harm caused by addictive product design. The decision centered on the companies’ use of persuasive technologies—such as infinite scroll, variable reward schedules, and personalized content feeds—to keep users engaged beyond reasonable limits. The court found that these design choices constituted a breach of duty of care, creating a precedent that design decisions can be actionable under consumer protection law. For enterprise IT teams, the ruling underscores that user experience (UX) and product design are not purely aesthetic or marketing concerns; they are now part of the legal risk profile. Enterprises that build internal tools, customer portals, or mobile apps must audit their design logic for potentially exploitative features, especially those that influence user behavior in ways that could be deemed harmful or addictive. Vendors such as Adobe Experience Manager, Salesforce Commerce Cloud, and Microsoft Power Apps, which provide low‑code development and content personalization, are directly implicated because they offer the building blocks for such features. The broader industry implication is a shift toward “responsible design” frameworks that embed ethical guidelines, user‑centered metrics, and compliance checks into the product lifecycle. Companies that fail to adapt risk litigation, regulatory fines, and reputational damage. The decision also signals that regulators may increasingly scrutinize design choices under existing consumer protection and privacy laws, such as the UK GDPR, which requires transparency about how data is used to influence user behavior. Enterprises should therefore integrate design ethics into their governance structures, ensuring that product teams are trained to recognize and mitigate potential harms. This will not only reduce legal exposure but also align with emerging industry standards, such as the EU’s Digital Services Act and the forthcoming US Federal Trade Commission guidelines on persuasive design.
