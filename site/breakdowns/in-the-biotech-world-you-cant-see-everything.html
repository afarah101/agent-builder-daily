Summary

The new documentary “You Can See Everything” released by MIT Technology Review examines the rise and collapse of Elizabeth Holmes, founder of Theranos, a biotech company that claimed to revolutionize blood testing with a proprietary microfluidic device. The film details how Theranos’s technology was never validated, how the company misled investors, regulators, and patients, and how Holmes was eventually charged with fraud. For enterprise IT teams, the story underscores the critical need for rigorous technical due diligence when evaluating vendor claims, especially in regulated sectors such as healthcare. The documentary highlights that Theranos relied on a combination of proprietary hardware, custom software, and cloud services that were never subjected to independent validation. IT teams must therefore audit the entire technology stack—hardware specifications, software source code, data pipelines, and cloud configurations—to ensure compliance with industry standards such as HIPAA, ISO/IEC 27001, and UK GDPR. Vendors that were involved or referenced include Illumina for sequencing, Amazon Web Services for cloud hosting, and various contract manufacturers for device components. The broader industry implication is a heightened scrutiny of biotech startups that promise disruptive diagnostics. Regulators are now demanding more transparent validation data, and investors are requiring proof of concept before funding. Enterprises that partner with such vendors must implement stricter vendor risk management, enforce data integrity checks, and maintain audit trails to mitigate legal and reputational risks. The documentary serves as a cautionary tale that the promise of rapid innovation can mask technical deficiencies, and that enterprise IT must enforce robust validation, compliance, and monitoring processes before integrating new biotech solutions.

How to apply

Evaluate

• Inventory all biotech-related assets: hardware, firmware, software, cloud services, and data flows. • Verify that each component has documented specifications, validation reports, and compliance certifications (e.g., FDA 21 CFR Part 820, ISO 13485). • Conduct a technical audit of the data pipeline: sample collection, processing, storage, and reporting. • Assess data governance: encryption at rest and in transit, access controls, audit logs, and retention policies. • Review third‑party vendor contracts for security and compliance clauses.

Propose

• Build a business case that quantifies risk reduction: e.g., “Implementing a validated data integrity framework reduces audit findings by 70 % and potential regulatory fines by £2 M annually.” • Benchmark against industry peers: use metrics such as mean time to detection (MTTD) for data anomalies, percentage of validated devices, and compliance audit pass rates. • Include cost estimates for tooling, personnel training, and process redesign. • Present a phased ROI: immediate compliance gains (0–3 months) and long‑term operational efficiencies (12–24 months).

Tools to consider

• Vendor Risk Management: LogicManager, RiskWatch, or RSA Archer. • Data Integrity & Validation: Evident, Medidata Rave, or OpenEHR for clinical data. • Cloud Security: AWS Config, Azure Security Center, or Google Cloud Security Command Center. • Compliance Automation: OneTrust (UK GDPR), TrustArc, or DataGrail. • Continuous Monitoring: Splunk, Elastic SIEM, or Datadog.

Risks to flag

• Technical: Unvalidated algorithms, lack of reproducibility, insecure firmware updates, and weak encryption. • Compliance: Failure to meet UK GDPR data subject rights, inadequate data breach notification procedures, and non‑compliance with GDPR data transfer mechanisms. • Operational: Single points of failure in sample processing, insufficient audit trails, and lack of disaster recovery plans.

Quick win

• Deploy a centralized audit log solution (e.g., Splunk or Elastic SIEM) to capture all biotech device events. • Conduct a rapid gap analysis against ISO/IEC 27001 controls and remediate the top three findings within 30 days.

Long-term play

• Over 6–12 months, establish a formal validation program: create a cross‑functional team (IT, QA, regulatory, clinical) to perform end‑to‑end testing of all biotech solutions before deployment. • Implement a continuous compliance framework that integrates automated policy checks, real‑time monitoring, and periodic third‑party audits. • Develop a vendor scorecard that tracks technical performance, compliance status, and incident history, feeding into procurement and risk management decisions.
