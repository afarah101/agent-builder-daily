Detailed summary

PC shipments fell 20.1 percent year‑over‑year in Q3 2026, the steepest decline since Q1 2023, according to Omdia and IDC. The drop is driven by a combination of high device prices, inventory surpluses, and a slowdown in corporate and consumer demand. For enterprise IT teams this signals a potential shift in the volume of new device deployments, which directly impacts MDM, endpoint security, and support operations. Vendors such as Dell, HP, Lenovo, and Acer have reported lower sales figures, while cloud‑based management platforms (Microsoft Intune, VMware Workspace ONE, Cisco Secure Endpoint) are seeing changes in subscription uptake. The broader industry implication is a possible re‑evaluation of procurement cycles, a tightening of supply chains, and a re‑allocation of budget toward software‑defined security rather than hardware refreshes. Enterprises should anticipate that the spike in inventory may lead to discounted hardware, but also to longer lead times and potential obsolescence risk for legacy devices. Monitoring ticket volumes for MDM and endpoint security over the next month will help detect whether the decline in shipments translates into fewer new device enrollments or, conversely, a backlog of pending upgrades. The trend also underscores the need for robust inventory management and a flexible licensing model that can scale with fluctuating device counts.

IT engineer application guide

Evaluate

- Audit current device inventory: count active, pending, and decommissioned units. - Review MDM enrollment logs for the past 90 days to establish baseline ticket rates. - Check licensing utilization for endpoint protection and management platforms. - Assess supply chain status: vendor lead times, inventory levels, and warranty coverage.

Propose

- Build a business case showing projected ticket load changes based on a 20 % reduction in new device deployments. - Use metrics: current ticket per device ratio, average resolution time, and cost per ticket. - Benchmark against industry averages (e.g., 0.5 tickets per device per quarter). - Highlight potential savings from deferred hardware purchases and increased focus on software licensing.

Tools to consider

- Microsoft Intune (MDM/EMM) for streamlined enrollment and policy management. - VMware Workspace ONE for unified endpoint management across Windows, macOS, and Linux. - Cisco Secure Endpoint (formerly AMP) for integrated threat detection and response. - ServiceNow ITSM for ticket tracking and analytics. - Lansweeper or Microsoft Endpoint Manager for asset discovery and inventory.

Risks to flag

- Technical: Compatibility issues with legacy OS versions, potential gaps in coverage if devices are decommissioned prematurely. - Compliance: Ensure that any changes to device handling or data retention comply with UK GDPR, especially when disposing of hardware. - Operational: Over‑reliance on a single vendor could create single‑point failure; maintain multi‑vendor strategy.

Quick win

- Implement a “device lifecycle” dashboard in ServiceNow that flags devices approaching end‑of‑support within 30 days. - Re‑allocate a small budget to extend endpoint protection licenses for existing devices, reducing the need for new hardware.

Long-term play

- Over 6–12 months, transition to a subscription‑based procurement model that ties hardware purchases to actual usage metrics. - Deploy a predictive analytics layer (e.g., using Power BI or Tableau) to forecast device demand based on ticket trends and market signals. - Negotiate volume‑based discounts with key vendors and lock in favorable terms for future refresh cycles.
