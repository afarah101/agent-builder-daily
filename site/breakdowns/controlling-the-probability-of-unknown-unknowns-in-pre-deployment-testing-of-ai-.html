Controlling the Probability of Unknown Unknowns in Pre-Deployment Testing of AI Systems

Rachel Li, Chris Hays et al. · 11 Nov 2026

Summary

The study by Li, Hays, Ramdas, and Raghavan introduces an anytime‑valid confidence sequence for estimating the probability of unseen failures in AI systems during pre‑deployment testing. Traditional fixed‑sample methods require a predetermined number of test runs and can either over‑test (wasting resources) or under‑test (missing rare failures). The authors construct a sequential bound that tightens as more test data accumulate, allowing engineers to stop testing once the upper bound on the failure probability falls below a pre‑set risk tolerance. They validate the approach on a real coding‑agent dataset, demonstrating that the confidence sequence achieves tighter bounds than conventional bootstrapping or Bayesian credible intervals, and that it can reduce the number of required test runs by up to 30% while maintaining a 95% confidence that the true failure rate is below the threshold.

For enterprise IT, this method directly addresses the challenge of scaling AI testing in regulated environments where the cost of a post‑deployment failure can be high. By providing a statistically rigorous stopping rule, teams can allocate testing resources more efficiently, reduce time‑to‑market, and maintain compliance with internal risk frameworks. The tighter bounds also mean fewer false positives in safety assessments, which can otherwise lead to unnecessary remediation cycles.

Real‑world implications include the ability to integrate the confidence sequence into continuous integration pipelines, enabling automated decision points for test completion. Organizations can set enterprise‑wide risk thresholds (e.g., 1% failure probability) and let the algorithm determine when sufficient evidence has been gathered. This reduces manual oversight, aligns testing effort with actual risk exposure, and provides auditable evidence for regulators. The approach is also adaptable to multi‑model or ensemble deployments, where unseen interactions can cause failures that are difficult to capture with static test suites.

Overall, the paper offers a practical, statistically sound tool that can be embedded in existing test harnesses, providing measurable savings in testing time and cost while preserving or improving safety guarantees for AI systems deployed at scale.
