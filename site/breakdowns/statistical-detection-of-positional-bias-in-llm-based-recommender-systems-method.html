Statistical Detection of Positional Bias in LLM-Based Recommender Systems: Methodologies and Empirical Evidence

Carlos Peláez-González, Francisco Herrera et al. · 27 Oct 2026

Summary

SECTION_SUMMARY Large Language Models are increasingly used as recommendation generators in conversational recommender systems via retrieval‑augmented generation. The study focuses on positional bias in top‑1 and single‑turn LLM‑based recommender systems, defined as the systematic dependence of the recommended item on the order in which candidates appear in the prompt. Two complementary detection methodologies were proposed: POB‑ICM, which uses contingency matrix analysis and Chi‑square independence testing, and POB‑UNBP, a sample‑efficient approach derived from a uniformity null model under permutation‑based evaluation that supports stochastic decoding with formal statistical guarantees.

A controlled RAG testbed for product recommendation was built on a real product catalog, a fixed prompt template, 2000 template‑generated user queries, and 20 permutations each, yielding 40,000 instances. Both methodologies rejected the null hypothesis of positional independence and uniformity in all evaluated state‑of‑the‑art models. Three complementary metrics were introduced—permutation sensitivity, positional bias, and strong positional bias—to quantify the effect, with the latter targeting cases where the position displaces the recommendation toward clearly less relevant items. A robustness analysis across candidate‑set size and decoding temperature demonstrated that the bias is structural and model‑dependent, not an artifact of deterministic decoding or candidate‑set size.

Our take: Routine auditing of candidate order and prompt design should become a standard governance practice for any cloud‑hosted LLM recommendation service, ensuring that enterprise deployments do not unintentionally favor certain items due to prompt structure.

Propose
