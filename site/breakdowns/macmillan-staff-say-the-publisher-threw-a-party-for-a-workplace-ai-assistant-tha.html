Detailed summary

In early 2024, Macmillan Publishers launched Chaptr’s NOA, a closed‑loop AI assistant designed to streamline editorial workflows and reduce manual data entry. NOA was integrated into the company’s content management system and accessed via a lightweight web interface. Within a year, the tool began to exhibit frequent hallucinations, mis‑retrieving proprietary metadata and generating editorial suggestions that conflicted with editorial standards. The incidents escalated when NOA inadvertently exposed confidential author contracts and unpublished manuscript details to internal users through its knowledge base. In response, the IT security team performed a rapid audit and discovered that NOA’s data ingestion pipeline lacked encryption at rest and did not enforce role‑based access controls. The vendor’s support team was slow to address the privacy gaps, and the tool’s performance degraded as the volume of content grew. By October 2025, Macmillan’s CIO announced the sunset of NOA, citing unsatisfactory accuracy, data privacy violations, and a lack of vendor roadmap alignment with the company’s compliance requirements. The organization pivoted to Google Gemini, a generative AI platform that offers tighter data‑control APIs and a more robust compliance framework, but the transition has been uneven; some teams still rely on legacy scripts that expose data to third‑party services.

For enterprise IT teams, this case underscores the critical importance of rigorous data‑handling audits before adopting AI assistants. The incident also highlights the risk of vendor lock‑in when a tool’s architecture is opaque and the vendor’s roadmap is unclear. The broader industry implication is a growing need for AI solutions that provide explicit data‑retention controls, end‑to‑end encryption, and transparent audit trails. Regulators are increasingly scrutinizing AI‑driven workflows for compliance with data protection laws such as UK GDPR, and vendors that fail to meet these standards risk losing enterprise customers. Consequently, IT leaders must prioritize tools that allow granular access policies, provide on‑premises or hybrid deployment options, and offer clear SLAs for data privacy and model reliability.

IT engineer application guide

Evaluate

- Inventory all AI‑enabled applications (chatbots, recommendation engines, automated tagging) and map their data flows. - Verify encryption at rest and in transit for all AI data stores; ensure role‑based access controls are enforced. - Run a hallucination test: feed controlled prompts and capture outputs; compare against ground truth. - Assess vendor SLAs for data deletion, model updates, and incident response times. - Conduct a compliance gap analysis against UK GDPR: data minimization, lawful basis, data subject rights.

Propose

- Build a cost‑benefit model: quantify time saved per editor (e.g., 2 hours/week) versus licensing and support costs. - Include risk mitigation metrics: reduction in data breach incidents, number of hallucination events per month. - Benchmark against industry peers: average AI accuracy (e.g., 95% precision) and compliance audit scores. - Present a phased rollout plan with pilot KPIs: user adoption rate, error rate, and ROI timeline.

Tools to consider

- Google Gemini (with Data‑Control APIs and on‑premises deployment options) - Microsoft Azure OpenAI Service (with Azure Policy integration for data governance) - Anthropic Claude (offers “Constitutional AI” for safer outputs) - OpenAI Enterprise (provides data‑retention controls and audit logs) - DataDog or Splunk for monitoring AI model outputs and detecting anomalies

Risks to flag

- Technical: model hallucinations, lack of explainability, insufficient data encryption. - Compliance: GDPR Article 5 (data minimization), Article 32 (security), Article 33 (breach notification). - Operational: vendor lock‑in, slow patch cycles, inadequate incident response. - Human‑error: improper training leading to misuse of AI outputs, overreliance on unverified suggestions.

Quick win

- Deploy an internal “AI Output Review” workflow: add a mandatory human review step for all AI‑generated editorial content before publication. - Configure a simple audit log that captures AI prompt, response, and user ID; store logs in a secure, immutable repository. - Run a one‑week pilot with a single editorial team to measure hallucination frequency and adjust prompt templates.

Long-term play

- Over 6–12 months, architect a hybrid AI platform that allows on‑premises inference for sensitive data while leveraging cloud models for general tasks. - Implement a governance framework: data‑classification policies, model‑audit schedules, and a “data‑first” design review for new AI features. - Establish a vendor‑management program that includes SLAs for data deletion, model versioning, and compliance attestations. - Build an internal AI center of excellence to standardize prompt engineering, model monitoring, and continuous training of staff on safe AI practices.
