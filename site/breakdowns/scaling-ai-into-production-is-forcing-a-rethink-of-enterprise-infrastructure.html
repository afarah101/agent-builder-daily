Scaling AI into production is forcing a rethink of enterprise infrastructure

Summary

Organizations are facing significant challenges in scaling AI from pilots and proofs of concept to production deployment across real workloads and business environments. This is forcing a rethink of enterprise infrastructure, highlighting the need for more flexible and scalable solutions. The main issue is that traditional infrastructure is not designed to handle the unique demands of AI workloads, which require large amounts of data, processing power, and memory. As a result, IT teams are struggling to deploy AI models in production, which is limiting their ability to realize the full benefits of AI. This matters for enterprise IT teams because it requires them to reassess their infrastructure and consider new solutions that can support AI deployments. Vendors such as NVIDIA, Google, and Amazon are involved in providing solutions for AI deployments, including cloud-based services, specialized hardware, and software frameworks. The broader industry implications are that companies will need to invest in new infrastructure and technologies to support AI deployments, which will require significant resources and planning. Additionally, there will be a need for new skills and expertise to manage and maintain AI systems, which will require IT teams to upskill and reskill. Overall, the shift to AI is driving a major transformation in enterprise infrastructure, and IT teams will need to be proactive in addressing the challenges and opportunities that this presents.

How to apply

Evaluate

Assess your current infrastructure to determine its ability to support AI deployments, including the capacity of your servers, storage, and network. Consider the types of AI workloads you will be running and the resources they will require. Evaluate your current virtualization platform and consider whether it can support the scalability and flexibility required for AI deployments.

Propose

Build a business case for investing in new infrastructure to support AI deployments, including the benefits of increased scalability, flexibility, and cost savings. Consider metrics such as reduced deployment time, increased model accuracy, and improved resource utilization. Propose a phased approach to implementing new infrastructure, starting with a small pilot project and scaling up to larger deployments.

Tools to consider

Consider vendors such as VMware, Microsoft, and Citrix for virtualization platforms, and NVIDIA and Google for specialized AI hardware and software. Also, consider cloud-based services such as Amazon SageMaker and Google Cloud AI Platform for deploying and managing AI models.

Risks to flag

Flag technical risks such as compatibility issues with existing infrastructure, and compliance risks such as ensuring that AI systems meet UK GDPR requirements. Also, flag operational risks such as ensuring that AI systems are properly monitored and maintained.

Quick win

Implement a small-scale AI pilot project using existing infrastructure to demonstrate the benefits of AI and identify potential issues. This can be achieved in under 30 days and will provide valuable insights and experience for larger deployments.

Long-term play

Develop a strategic plan for deploying AI across the enterprise, including investing in new infrastructure, upskilling IT staff, and developing new processes and procedures for managing AI systems. This will require a 6-12 month timeframe and will involve significant resources and planning, but will provide long-term benefits and competitive advantage.
