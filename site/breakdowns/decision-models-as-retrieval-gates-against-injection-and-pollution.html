Decision Models as Retrieval Gates Against Injection and Pollution

Mahdi Ghadamyari, Reza Habibi · Academic Institution · 2026-11-05 · Generated 07 Nov 2026, 12:47

Detailed summary

The study by Ghadamyari and Habibi evaluates a lightweight decision model, referred to as Kev-4B, designed to act as a retrieval gate that filters malicious or misleading content before it reaches a language model. The authors implemented the gate to scan HTML content and evaluate whether a prompt could be used to inject or pollute the model’s output. In controlled experiments, the Kev-4B gate reduced successful injection attacks from 27.9 % to 5.7 % while maintaining a false‑positive rate below 1 %. The gate processes each page in 76 ms on commodity hardware, indicating that the overhead is negligible compared to the cost of a full model inference. The authors argue that this approach offers a cheaper, faster defense than traditional prompt‑engineering or model‑level mitigations, and that it preserves the performance of downstream applications.

For enterprise IT, the findings are significant because they provide a practical, low‑cost layer of protection that can be deployed on existing web‑content pipelines. The gate can be integrated into content‑delivery networks, API gateways, or internal data‑injection points, allowing organizations to filter out potentially dangerous prompts before they reach a generative model. The reduction in injection success translates directly into fewer data leaks, fewer policy violations, and lower compliance risk. Moreover, the minimal latency impact means that user experience is largely unaffected, a key requirement for production systems that rely on real‑time AI responses.

Real‑world implications include the ability to enforce stricter content‑security policies without sacrificing performance, the opportunity to meet regulatory requirements such as UK GDPR by preventing inadvertent disclosure of personal data, and the potential to reduce the cost of model hosting by limiting the number of unsafe inferences. Enterprises can adopt this gate as part of a layered defense strategy, complementing existing input‑validation and monitoring tools. The study demonstrates that a simple, rule‑based decision model can provide measurable security benefits at scale, making it a compelling addition to any AI‑driven product portfolio.
