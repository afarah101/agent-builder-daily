Make the Adversary Spend More: Stateful Deception as a Cost-Exchange Primitive for AI-Era Cyber Defense

Alessandro Cantelli-Forti, Isabella Marasco, Hosam Alamleh, Giada Boschi · Academic Institution · 2026-11-25 · Generated 27 Nov 2026, 11:27

Summary

The study evaluates a stateful deception platform that uses a local language model to maintain consistent attacker interactions across multiple SSH sessions. Researchers deployed a honeypot that records each attacker’s actions and tailors subsequent responses to preserve the illusion of a live system. They compared this approach to a stateless LLM‑driven decoy and found that the stateful system reduced defender effort by 63 % while increasing the time attackers spent on the honeypot. The reduction in effort was measured in terms of monitoring, incident response hours, and manual triage, whereas the increased attacker time was quantified through session duration and number of failed login attempts. The study also reported that attackers were more likely to reveal reconnaissance data and credentials when the deception maintained a coherent narrative, enabling defenders to gather actionable threat intelligence.

For enterprise IT, this demonstrates that a well‑designed deception layer can act as a cost‑exchange primitive: defenders invest relatively little in maintaining a persistent decoy, but attackers expend disproportionate effort and resources. The findings are directly applicable to organizations that rely on SSH for remote administration, as the honeypot can be integrated into existing bastion hosts without significant architectural changes. By forcing attackers to waste time, the organization gains a buffer that can be used to detect, analyze, and remediate threats before they reach production systems. Additionally, the study highlights the importance of stateful interaction for credibility; attackers are less likely to suspect a decoy if the system remembers prior actions, which is a key insight for designing future deception tools.

Real‑world implications include the potential to reduce the overall cost of incident response, lower the risk of credential compromise, and provide a richer dataset for threat hunting. Enterprises can deploy such a system as part of a broader security operations center (SOC) strategy, using the captured attacker data to improve detection rules and harden real servers. The approach also offers a measurable return on investment: a 63 % reduction in defender effort translates to significant savings in manpower and tooling, while the increased attacker dwell time can be leveraged for threat intelligence and compliance reporting. The study provides a concrete, actionable blueprint for organizations looking to add a low‑cost, high‑impact layer to their defensive posture.
