Detailed Summary

Expedia's AI chief Xavi Amatriain recently suggested that evals could replace traditional product requirements documents (PRDs) and product design documents. This approach involves encoding product requirements and security through evals, including red teaming evals, before coding begins. This shift-left security approach embeds security requirements early in the development cycle, potentially leading to better security postures. For enterprise IT teams, this matters as it highlights the importance of incorporating security into the design phase, rather than treating it as an afterthought. The involvement of AI-driven red teaming exercises can also help identify vulnerabilities earlier on. While no specific vendors or technologies were mentioned, the broader industry implication is a move towards more proactive and integrated security measures. This approach can help reduce the risk of security breaches and improve overall product quality. Enterprise IT teams should review their current PRD and product design documents to assess where they can embed security evaluations earlier in the development cycle. By doing so, they can improve their security posture and reduce the risk of security breaches. The use of AI-driven red teaming exercises can also help identify vulnerabilities and improve the overall security of products.

Consider using tools such as Veracode, Checkmarx, or Synopsys to support your evals and red teaming exercises. These tools can help identify vulnerabilities and improve the overall security of your products.

Compliance risks such as UK GDPR should also be considered, as well as operational risks such as the potential for false positives or negatives.
