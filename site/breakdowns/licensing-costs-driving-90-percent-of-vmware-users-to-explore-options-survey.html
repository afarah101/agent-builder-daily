Summary

SECTION_SUMMARY The 2026 IT Virtualization Survey conducted by Rimini Street, published by Ars Technica, reports that 90% of organizations using VMware are actively evaluating alternative virtualization or cloud‑native solutions. The primary driver is the steep rise in VMware licensing fees, compounded by Broadcom’s recent policy changes that have tightened renewal terms and introduced higher per‑CPU costs. The survey also identifies operational complexity, the need to manage multiple vendors, and an expanding attack surface as significant impediments to migration.

For enterprise IT teams, this trend signals a critical reassessment of the total cost of ownership (TCO) for VMware environments. The cost pressure forces teams to scrutinize not only license fees but also the hidden expenses of patch management, support contracts, and the overhead of maintaining a legacy stack that may not scale efficiently in a hybrid or multi‑cloud context.

Key vendors and technologies mentioned include VMware (Broadcom), Rimini Street (as a cost‑saving alternative), and cloud‑native platforms such as Google Cloud VMware Engine, Microsoft Azure VMware Solution, and Red Hat OpenShift. The broader industry implication is a shift toward “lift‑and‑shift” or re‑architected workloads on managed services that promise lower operational overhead and a reduced attack surface. The survey underscores the need for a more agile, cost‑effective virtualization strategy that can adapt to rapid changes in licensing and market dynamics.

The findings also highlight a growing appetite for multi‑vendor or hybrid approaches, where organizations can mix on‑prem, public cloud, and managed services to optimize performance, cost, and security. As a result, IT leaders must balance the benefits of VMware’s mature feature set against the financial and operational risks identified in the survey. The industry is moving toward a more competitive virtualization landscape, with vendors offering subscription‑based models, pay‑per‑use pricing, and integrated security services to attract cost‑conscious enterprises.
