Detailed summary

The study conducted a controlled experimental evaluation of two popular large language model (LLM) serving engines, vLLM and SGLang, within a Kubernetes testbed. Researchers focused on how each engine handles the prefill phase for long prompts—a critical bottleneck in inference latency. By aligning a key configuration parameter, “chunked prefill,” across both engines, they eliminated the largest performance disparities that previously existed. The experiments revealed that, when configured identically, the engines still differed in tail‑latency behavior, with cache‑aware engines consistently delivering lower worst‑case latency. Quantitatively, the tuned configuration reduced the 99th‑percentile latency by up to 6× compared to default settings. For enterprise IT, this finding is significant because LLM services are increasingly deployed at scale in production environments where SLA guarantees on latency and cost predictability are mandatory. The ability to reduce tail latency through a single, well‑understood configuration tweak means that existing infrastructure can deliver higher quality of service without costly hardware upgrades or architectural overhauls. Additionally, the study demonstrates that performance is not solely a function of the underlying engine but also of how it is tuned; therefore, performance testing and continuous monitoring should become part of the standard operating procedure for LLM deployments. In practice, organizations can now benchmark their own deployments against the study’s baseline, identify misconfigurations, and apply the chunked prefill setting to achieve measurable latency gains. This directly translates to better user experience, lower operational costs, and stronger compliance with SLAs in regulated industries.
