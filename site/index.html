Cache Engineering for Agents

Tianyu Fu, Ruoxi Wang, Zihan Min, Zhongqi Ren · NYU · 20 Oct 2026

The research tackles the rising cost of key‑value cache I/O and storage as LLM agents tackle long action‑observation sequences, and it shows a way to control and shrink the active cache without discarding useful history. The authors built a cache‑engineering technique that trims or summarizes the trajectory while keeping subsequent states valid, reducing the need for costly re‑prefilling. As a result, agents can operate longer sequences with lower I/O overhead and maintain accuracy.

HOW TO APPLY THIS

If you manage cloud infrastructure that hosts large language models or AI agents, apply this: 1) monitor cache growth in your deployments; 2) integrate the paper’s trimming logic to keep the cache below the model’s context limit; 3) test that re‑prefilling rates drop and latency improves. If your workloads do not use LLM agents, this is not directly applicable.
