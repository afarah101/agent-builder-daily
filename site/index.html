Inject a Turn, Not a Token: Learning Where to Redirect LLM Reasoning for Self-Correction

Jiwon Jeon, Myungsik Cho, Jeonghye Kim, Youngchul Sung · Academic Institution · 09 Oct 2026

This study tackles the issue of large language models (LLMs) failing to spot and fix their own errors. The authors trained a small neural head on a frozen LLM to identify where to intervene in a reasoning chain and found that asking the model to continue in a fresh user turn is more effective than injecting instructions inline. The result shows that strategic redirection can improve LLM self‑correction, but it has limited relevance to day‑to‑day cloud infrastructure work.

A Society of Researchers: Designing Institutions for Populations of Autonomous Research Agents

Ali Asaria, Deep Gandhi, Tony Salomone · Academic Institution · 07 Oct 2026

Large-scale deployments of autonomous research agents often share a single compute pool but lack formal organisation, leading to inefficiency and governance gaps. This research proposes a 'society of agents'—a population of persistent agents governed by explicit institutions built on six guiding principles, demonstrated within a scientific research context. The result is a reusable framework that structures agent interactions and resource allocation more predictably.

HOW TO APPLY THIS

If you manage a shared compute pool for thousands of automated tasks, step 1: design explicit institutional rules that dictate agent lifecycles and resource quotas; step 2: implement a governance layer that monitors compliance with those rules; step 3: enforce the rules through automated enforcement mechanisms (e.g., quota throttling, job scheduling policies).
