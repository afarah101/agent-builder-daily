FastOMOP: A Foundational Architecture for Reliable Agentic Real-World Evidence Generation on OMOP CDM data

Niko Moeller-Grell, Shihao Shenzhang, Zhangshu Joshua Jiang, Richard JB Dobson · Academic Institution · 2026-04-27

The Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership Common Data Model (OMOP CDM) has harmonized electronic health records data of nearly one billion patients, but generating real-world evidence from these repositories remains a manual process. The proposed FastOMOP architecture utilizes AI agents and Large Language Models (LLMs) to automate the process of generating real-world evidence. The outcome of this approach is the potential to increase efficiency and reduce the need for manual expertise in clinical and epidemiological tasks.

HOW TO APPLY THIS

To apply this approach, AI practitioners can leverage the OMOP CDM to develop and train AI models that can automate the process of generating real-world evidence. By utilizing AI agents and LLMs, practitioners can create more efficient and scalable solutions for real-world evidence generation.

BUSINESS OPPORTUNITY

The FastOMOP architecture presents a clear product opportunity for companies looking to develop automated solutions for real-world evidence generation. By commercializing this technology, companies can offer valuable services to healthcare organizations and pharmaceutical companies, enabling them to make more informed decisions with data-driven insights.

GradMAP: Gradient-Based Multi-Agent Proximal Learning for Grid-Edge Flexibility

Yihong Zhou, Hongtai Zeng, Thomas Morstyn · Academic Institution · 2026-04-27

The problem of coordinating large populations of grid-edge devices is addressed through a decentralized learning approach. The proposed GradMAP method uses gradient-based multi-agent proximal learning to train independent neural-network policies for each agent. This approach enables online decision-making without communication, respecting three-phase AC distribution-network physics.

HOW TO APPLY THIS

To apply this approach, AI practitioners can utilize gradient-based methods to train decentralized agents, allowing for efficient decision-making in complex systems. By embedding differentiable power-flow models, practitioners can ensure that their solutions respect the underlying physics of the system.

BUSINESS OPPORTUNITY

The GradMAP method presents a clear opportunity for product development in the energy sector, particularly in the management of grid-edge devices. A company could leverage this approach to create a decentralized energy management system, offering a competitive solution for utilities and grid operators.

Learning to Think from Multiple Thinkers

Nirmit Joshi, Roey Magen, Nathan Srebro, Nikolaos Tsilivis · MIT · 2026-04-27

The problem addressed in this study is the challenge of learning from Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervision from multiple thinkers, which can provide systematically different solutions to the same problem. The AI approach involves analyzing the feasibility of learning with CoT supervision from multiple thinkers and establishing the hardness of learning under cryptographic assumptions. The outcome of this study provides insights into the limitations of CoT supervision and its potential applications in various domains.

HOW TO APPLY THIS

To apply the findings of this study, AI practitioners should consider the potential biases and limitations of CoT supervision from multiple thinkers when designing learning models. By understanding the hardness of learning from CoT supervision, practitioners can develop more effective and robust learning strategies that account for the variability in human thinking.

BUSINESS OPPORTUNITY

The study's findings have potential applications in areas such as education and workforce training, where personalized learning models can be developed to account for individual differences in thinking and problem-solving. A concrete business opportunity arising from this research could be the development of AI-powered adaptive learning platforms that incorporate CoT supervision from multiple thinkers to provide more effective and personalized learning experiences.

Green Shielding: A User-Centric Approach Towards Trustworthy AI

Aaron J. Li, Nicolas Sanchez, Hao Huang, Ruijiang Dong · Academic Institution · 2026-04-27

The problem of large language models being sensitive to variations in user queries is addressed in this study. The proposed AI approach, Green Shielding, aims to characterize how benign input variation shifts model behavior through the CUE criteria. The outcome is a user-centric agenda for building evidence-backed deployment guidance, contributing to more trustworthy AI systems.

HOW TO APPLY THIS

To apply the Green Shielding approach, AI practitioners should focus on characterizing the impact of realistic input variations on model behavior, using benchmarks with authentic context and reference standards. By doing so, they can develop more robust and user-centric AI systems that are less sensitive to routine query variations.

BUSINESS OPPORTUNITY

A potential business opportunity arising from this research is the development of AI-powered tools that can automatically assess and improve the robustness of large language models, offering a competitive advantage to companies deploying these models. Such tools could be marketed as a service to AI developers and organizations, providing a concrete monetization opportunity for the Green Shielding approach.
