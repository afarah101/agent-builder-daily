Automated Safety Analysis for Agentic Workflows

Yanju Chen, Ying Li, Bosi Zhang, Hanzhi Liu·21 Nov 2026

This research tackles the problem of hidden logical errors in agentic LLM workflows that can cause real‑world harm. The authors built Apsis, a static safety analyzer that lifts workflow code into a common intermediate representation and matches declarative bug patterns, achieving 91% precision, 84% recall, and finding 36 new bugs.

How to apply: If you manage cloud infrastructure that hosts or supports LLM agentic workflows, do the following: 1) integrate Apsis into your CI pipeline to run static checks on each workflow before deployment; 2) review the IR reports to see which steps trigger flagged patterns; 3) patch those steps and re‑run the analyzer to confirm fixes.

When Does Decomposition Help? Empirical Study on Workload-Architecture Affinity in LLM Inference Serving

Jihui Yang, Wentai Wu, Jiahua Huang, Weiwei Lin·22 Nov 2026

The study shows that breaking down LLM inference into separate prefill and decode stages can reduce latency for large‑prefill workloads, but it hurts performance for short chat or heavy concurrent traffic. They compared five deployment styles on identical hardware and found single‑GPU replicas were best for most traffic, while pipeline parallelism only helped when prefill time dominated. If you manage LLM serving, this means you should match your deployment style to the workload: use replication for chat, pipeline or hybrid for 32K‑prefill tasks.

How to apply: If you manage LLM inference, check your workload type: for short‑chat or high‑concurrency traffic, deploy independent one‑GPU replicas; for 32K prefill‑heavy loads, use pipeline parallelism or a hybrid pipeline‑prefill/decode setup; avoid static tensor‑parallel splits that cannot scale phases independently.

Do LLM Agents Compromise Like Humans? Risk and Responsibility in Dyadic Decision-Making

Noah S. Okada, Ketika Garg, Wenning Deng, Dean Mobbs·21 Nov 2026

The study shows that LLM agents can mimic human-like compromise in joint decisions, but they do it differently—mainly by focusing on rewards rather than reciprocal adjustments and by attributing responsibility in a way that is not linked to partner behaviour. This suggests that AI partners may not learn to share responsibility in the same way humans do, potentially leading to misaligned expectations in collaborative systems.

How to apply: If you manage cloud infrastructure that uses LLM agents for decision support, check that the agents’ risk preferences are aligned with your SLA requirements, monitor their reward‑driven choices for bias, and verify that responsibility attribution is clearly defined in your operational playbooks.
