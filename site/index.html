Scaffold, Not Vocabulary? A Controlled, Two-Tier, Pre-Registered Study of a Popperian Code-Generation Skill

Mehmet Iscan · Academic Institution · 04 Jun 2026

This research explores how large language models can be used to generate code and investigates whether the improvement in generated code is due to the model's ability to reason like a scientist or the structure of the prompts used. The study built a controlled experiment to test this and found that the structure of the prompts has a significant impact on the quality of the generated code. The result shows that the scaffold used in prompts is more important than the specific skill or vocabulary used.

HOW TO APPLY THIS

If you manage AI-powered code generation tools, here is what to do: review your prompt structures to ensure they are well-designed, check your language models for positional and self-preference biases, and consider implementing a two-tier testing approach to validate the quality of generated code. This flags a risk: if your org uses large language models for code generation, check your prompt structures immediately to avoid potential biases. Not directly applicable to traditional IT infrastructure work, but relevant to those managing AI-powered code generation tools.
